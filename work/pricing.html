Spare parts pricing · CRM solution

An automated and centralised CRM solution for spare parts pricing and price request handling.

Context

The company supports thousands of spare parts for chip-making tools. Setting the right price, and keeping it consistent across teams, was slow and manual.

The problem

Pricing lived in silos

Three teams, and many separate sites

Pricing, product and sales teams worked in silos, across 3 to 4 separate SharePoint sites. They managed thousands of spare parts. We looked at pricing tools on the market, but most were for retail, and pushing data to our ERP system was not technically possible.

We had to buy or build

The company wanted to unify the experience, so we decided to design it in-house. I was asked to design this end-to-end product in 45 days, including user testing.

My process

A five-day design sprint

I ran the sprint with the whole team, then we continued with weekly sprints.

  1. Discover

    Stakeholder, user and technical interviews

  2. Define

    Key audiences, user stories and journey map

  3. Design

    Crazy 8s, user flow, sitemap and paper sketches

  4. Prototype

    Clickable prototype to tell the story

  5. Test

    Weekly feedback from real users

The decisions

What I decided, and why

The tool suggests a price, and people can change it

Analysts trusted their experience more than a model. A fully automatic price would not work. So the tool suggests a model price, and the analyst can override it and send it for review.

People stay responsible for the final price.

One flow for three teams

The price analyst works on daily requests. The product manager reviews it and compares similar parts. Then it goes to the ERP system and the sales price book, with renew and expiry dates.

I kept the main screen simple

There was a lot of complex information. I found what users need most to see on the screen, and kept the rest one click away.

The prototype made people agree

User flows were hard to discuss in meetings. They finally clicked for stakeholders when they played with my prototype.

Context

Each quarter, regions forecast how many field engineers they will need. Field service productivity has a direct impact on revenue, so this planning is important for the business.

The problem

Six to eight weeks of spreadsheets

Every region used its own numbers

There was no standard process to plan headcount. Different teams used different calculations and methods, so the work was long and manual, and leaders could not compare numbers.

Moving engineers between regions was also manual

The global loan labour process was tracked in Excel, with no closed loop between the requester, regional focal, planning team and managers. It was hard to see available people and matching opportunities.

My process

How I worked

  1. Understand

    Learn how planners and leaders do the work today

  2. Set the goal

    Agree on one standard model and clear success metrics

  3. Design

    Calculator, dashboard and approval flow

  4. Improve

    Add what people needed in version 2.0

The decisions

What I decided, and why

I made "what if" the centre of the tool

Planners needed to test scenarios, not only fill in numbers. So I designed an automated headcount calculator with what-if scenarios. I did not want to move the same spreadsheet online.

Planning became a discussion about options.

I designed approvals that follow the organisation

Requests were going back and forth over email. So I designed a multi-level approval flow, with role-based access and numbers that roll up the hierarchy. Each leader sees and approves their own level, and can drill down.

I gave leaders one standard view

In version 2.0 I added a summary of headcount metrics, quarter by quarter comparison, interactive KPI charts, drill through by owner, and download to Excel for people who still need it.

What I learned

Standardise first, then automate.

If each team uses different maths, a new screen will not help. We first had to agree on one model.
